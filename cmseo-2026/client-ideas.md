# Client ideas from CMSEO 2026

These are first drafts to sort through after the conference.

## From Alex Moss: AI traffic and agent readiness

- **AI traffic quick audit (cheap to deliver):**
  - GA4 report on AI sources: chatgpt.com, perplexity, copilot, gemini.
  - Log check for `ChatGPT-User` to find which pages people ask ChatGPT about.
  - Turn the findings into a priority page list for each client.
- **Enquiry form update:** add "ChatGPT or another AI assistant" to the "How did you find us?" options on every client's forms. This captures AI-driven leads that analytics misses.
- **Facts as text:** for product and service clients, put specs, pricing basis, minimum orders, lead times and service areas on the page as plain text. Not images, PDFs or configurators.
- **Agent-ready basics:** `llms.txt`, Markdown copies of pages, a Content-Signal line in `robots.txt`, and clean schema.
  - **Caveat from his data:** this gets the brand described correctly, but doesn't win citations for general facts. Authorities such as GOV.UK, the BBC and Wikipedia get those.
  - **Pitch it as:** making sure AI describes you correctly, not as a way to get cited.
- **Original data wins citations:** to get cited, publish facts nobody else has, such as client surveys, pricing data and case-study numbers.

## From Victoria Olsina: one post, many channels

- **Productised repurposing pipeline:** each client blog post becomes:
  - a LinkedIn and X post
  - a LinkedIn or Medium article
  - a branded carousel
  - a short talking-head video
- **How it fits with Alex's finding:** AI cites authorities for facts, so spreading a client's own expertise across platforms AI reads (LinkedIn, Medium, YouTube) builds the brand mentions that count.
- **Watch-outs:**
  - **Duplicate content:** Medium's "Import a story" sets a canonical link back to the original. LinkedIn articles have no canonical, so rewrite the angle there rather than pasting the post.
  - **AI avatar video:** get the client's consent, use a real spokesperson's likeness, and consider disclosing that it's AI-made.
  - **Accuracy:** a human reviews before anything is published.
- **Possible pilot:** take one existing client blog post, run it through all four lines, and show the team the output, time taken and cost.

## From Will Tribe: many sites from one source

- **One source of truth, many sites:** keep business data, offers, schema, pricing and FAQs in one central place, and push updates to every site automatically.
- **Who it suits:**
  - multi-location and franchise clients
  - clients with several brands
  - lead-gen sites the agency owns
- **Combine with Alex Moss's checklist:** generate `llms.txt`, Markdown copies, schema and Content-Signal for every site from the same source.
- **Test before scaling:** cheap AI-built test sites with clear go or kill rules, such as calls per month, before investing in a niche.
- **Risk to manage:** Google's scaled content abuse and doorway page policies. Each site needs genuinely useful content of its own, not template swaps.

## Carlos's idea: writer voice models and blended house styles

**The idea:**
- Feed AI a real writer's work so it learns their language and style.
- Or blend 2 or 3 writers into one consolidated voice, keeping their quirks, opinions and biases.
- The goal is content that reads as human and trustworthy, not machine-made.
- Tune the voice to the audience. A fuel card brand and a mattress review site need very different voices.

**How to build it:**

1. **Collect samples:** 10 to 30 pieces per writer, plus emails and call transcripts. Unpolished writing shows the real voice best.
2. **Write a voice card:** extract a one-page style guide covering:
   - sentence length and rhythm
   - favourite words and phrases, and words they never use
   - how they open and close a piece
   - how they show doubt
   - their opinions and pet peeves
   - their humour
3. **Blend (optional):** compare the cards and choose which traits to keep from each person.
4. **Add an audience card:** who the reader is, what they already know, what they distrust and which proof they need.
5. **Draft and review:** AI drafts using voice card + audience card + 3 to 5 real examples. A real person edits and signs off.
6. **Blind test:** mix AI drafts with real pieces and see whether the team can tell which is which.

**Audience examples:**

| | Fuel card (B2B) | Mattress reviews (B2C) |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Fleet manager or business owner | Tired shopper comparing options |
| Wants | Cost per litre, fees, tax, admin time, compliance | First-hand feel, sleep position, heat, returns |
| Trusts | Numbers, specifics, a named expert | Real testing evidence, honest negatives, photos |
| Voice | Plain, precise, numbers first | Conversational, sensory, opinionated |

**Where the line is:**

- **Low risk:**
  - a house style blended from several writers, published under a brand byline
  - a real person's voice, used with their consent and approved by them
  - an obvious brand character, like a mascot
- **High risk:** an invented person with a fake bio or photo who claims first-hand experience. For example, "I slept on this mattress for 30 nights" when nobody did.
  - That's misleading conduct under Australian Consumer Law, and the ACCC actively pursues fake reviews.
  - It goes against Google's product review guidance.
  - Sports Illustrated was caught in 2023 publishing fake AI authors with fake headshots.

**SEO angle (links to Alex Moss's talk):** a made-up persona has no real-world presence, so it can never build author authority. A real expert can, through a LinkedIn history, podcast appearances and mentions on other sites.

**Make it feel human with real material, not planted typos:**
- specific details
- genuine opinions
- admitted downsides
- saying "it depends" where it does

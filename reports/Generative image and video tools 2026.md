# Rent the pixels, keep the proof real

As of 8 November 2026, the best approach for a small Australian agency is a **real-first, generate-around pipeline**. Real photos and phone footage carry every trust claim. Generative models supply plates, b-roll, cutouts, cleanups and illustrative explainers. Every word, logo, price and review is rendered as live type in code, and a grade-and-grain finishing pass keeps the AI parts from reading as "AI". On tools: **do not commit to an annual Higgsfield Ultra plan to catch the "buy until Nov 9" promotion.** Its unlimited items work only on the web, run in a slower queue, never apply to MCP runs from Claude Code, and mostly cover mid-ranked image models whose images cost cents anyway. Higgsfield, Magnific, Runway and the APIs all resell the same Kling, Seedance, Veo and Nano Banana models, so changing platform does not lower quality; it only changes how usage is billed. For a bursty two-client workload, the better billing model is **a Magnific plan with credits valid for 12 months** (Plus at US$33.75 a month or Pro at US$82.50 a month, both billed annually) as the main seat, plus pay-per-second APIs for Seedance and Kling 4.0. Stay on Ultra only if one measured billing cycle shows you using about 3,000 credits a month, mostly on Seedance or Kling 4.0. Australia still has no law requiring AI disclosure in ads. But the Australian Consumer Law's misleading-conduct rules now carry corporate penalties of up to **A$100M** for conduct from 28 March 2026, and Meta, Google and TikTok increasingly label AI ads automatically. The working rule for Alphalink is therefore simple: anything that implies "this is us" or "we did this" must be real. For City Rock y Ramen, the quickest quality gain is to stop stitching three separate generations together with AI morphs. Instead, animate the supplied poster in layers and join shots with designed, beat-synced transitions or a single keyframed generation.

## The Nov 9 Higgsfield promo buys web-only extras, not cheaper usage

The live Higgsfield connector shows the account on **Ultra, in an individual workspace** (8 Nov 2026). Ultra is listed at **US$99 a month billed annually** (US$1,188 a year), against US$129 billed monthly. It includes **3,000 credits a month** and up to eight parallel video and eight parallel image jobs ([Higgsfield MCP pricing config](https://higgsfield.ai/mcp-pricing)). The catch is in the expiry rules. Monthly credits are forfeited at renewal. Top-ups cost US$30 per 500 credits up to US$230 per 4,000, which is about **US$0.058–0.060 a credit, roughly 75% more than plan credits**, and they expire after 90 days ([Higgsfield MCP credits](https://higgsfield.ai/mcp-credits); [Segmind](https://blog.segmind.com/higgsfield-credits-explained-expiry-rules-and-real-cost/)). If every credit is used, annual Ultra costs about US$0.033 a credit, enough to buy a five-second 1080p Kling 4.0 clip for about US$0.54. At 60% use the effective rate rises to about US$0.055 a credit, which is top-up pricing.

The promotions are real but narrow. Every current Unlimited item is marked **"Buy until: Nov 9"** and "Available on web":
- **7-day unlimited:** Nano Banana 2 and Nano Banana Pro at 1K/2K. The annual card adds Kling 3.0 at 720p/5 s.
- **365-day unlimited:** Seedream 4.5, Seedream 5.0 Lite, FLUX.2 Pro at 1K, the original Nano Banana, Kling O1 Image and "GPT Image".
- **Free generations:** 5,000 (monthly) or 10,000 (annual) of Higgsfield's Soul V2 and Soul Cinema ([Higgsfield MCP pricing config](https://higgsfield.ai/mcp-pricing)).

Krea, a competitor, reports that MCP, CLI and Canvas runs still deduct credits while Unlimited is active, and that "dynamic speed adjustments" apply under load ([Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog/higgsfield-pricing-explained-2026-unlimited-credits-and-real-monthly-costs)). Users describe being "throttled at 15min per gen" on paid unlimited packs ([aiimagetovideo.pro](https://aiimagetovideo.pro/blog/?p=1341)). Higgsfield's own Trustpilot replies say unlimited runs through "the standard queue and speed may vary" ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/higgsfield.ai?page=4)). In practice, none of the unlimited usage can feed the agency's Claude Code studio.

The value is also thin. The 365-day list is made up of models that the only systematic realism test found flagged for exactly the tells this agency wants to avoid: Seedream 5.0 Lite showed "soft AI-like skin, mangled hands, typos", and FLUX.2 [pro] showed waxy skin. The current leaders, GPT Image 2.5/2.6 and Nano Banana 2.1, are not on the list, and the "GPT Image" entry names no version ([getimg.ai](https://getimg.ai/blog/ai-realism-test-comparing-realistic-image-generation-models)). Images are cheap even without the promotion. A hundred 2K Nano Banana Pro images cost about US$6.60 in Ultra credits, and Nano Banana 2.1 costs US$0.0252 per 2K image through Google's batch API ([Google AI Studio pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)). At this agency's volumes, the promotion is worth tens of dollars a month.

Set against that small saving is a 12-month commitment to a vendor with these risks:
- **Frequent repricing.** It renamed and repriced its plans at least twice between August and October 2026 ([Segmind](https://blog.segmind.com/higgsfield-credits-explained-expiry-rules-and-real-cost/); [usagepricing.com](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/higgsfield-2026-08-14-basic-pro-max-reprice)).
- **Credits can change value.** Its terms let it change what credits buy "at its sole discretion" with no notice period ([Segmind](https://blog.segmind.com/higgsfield-credits-explained-expiry-rules-and-real-cost/)).
- **Billing complaints.** These include auto-renewals, preselected annual billing and refunds described as "an obstacle course" ([MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/higgsfield-subscription-cancellation-complaints)).
- **Negative coverage in Australia.** Forbes Australia ran the headline "Racist videos and payment problems" ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/?p=177110)).
- **Training on uploads.** Its help centre says it **may train on user content outside the Enterprise plan**, and content cannot be removed from models already trained. This matters before anyone uploads Jimmy's face. IP indemnity is offered **on Enterprise only** ([Higgsfield help centre](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/account-and-privacy/who-owns-my-generations-and-can-i-use-them-commercially)).

The decision depends on which Ultra billing the account is on, which the connector does not show.

**If the plan bills monthly:** do not convert to annual before Nov 9. Run out the current cycle and pull `balance` and `transactions` through the Higgsfield MCP to measure the real monthly burn. Then move the main seat at renewal.

**If the plan is already annual:** the credits are paid for. Spend them first through the MCP on the jobs where Higgsfield is cheapest, turn off auto-renew and set the exit review a month before renewal. Higgsfield is genuinely cheapest for **Seedance 2.0 at 1080p (about US$1.49 per five seconds, against US$2.31 on Magnific and US$3.41 on fal)** and for Kling 4.0, but only when every credit is used ([Higgsfield blog](https://geo.higgsfield.ai/blog/veo-3-1-cost-and-limits); [Magnific pricing](https://www.magnific.com/pricing); [fal Seedance 2.0](https://fal.ai/models/bytedance/seedance-2.0/text-to-video/llms.txt)).

Switching does not cost quality, because the aggregators sell the same engines:
- **Higgsfield's catalogue** includes Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5, Wan 3.0, Gemini Omni, FLUX 3 video and MiniMax H3 ([Higgsfield MCP models_explore](https://higgsfield.ai)).
- **Magnific** quotes exact credit costs for Kling 3.0/4.0, Veo 3.1, Seedance 2.0 Pro and Nano Banana ([Magnific pricing](https://www.magnific.com/pricing)).
- **Runway** hosts Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5, Omni, H3 and Wan 3.0 ([Runway changelog](https://runway.com/changelog)).

What makes work look professional is the choice of model and the craft around it. Higgsfield-only extras such as Soul, Cinema Studio and the virality predictor are nice to have, but they are not decisive for documentary trades work.

## Seedance, Kling and Nano Banana 2.1 set the 2026 quality bar

The model landscape changed sharply in 2026:
- **Sora is gone.** OpenAI closed the app on 26 April and the API on 24 September, so it should be removed from all planning ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152-what-to-know-about-the-sora-discontinuation)).
- **Veo is legacy on the Gemini API.** Google shut the Veo 3.1 preview endpoints there on 22 October and named **Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash** (generally available from 5 November) as the replacement for every Veo model on that API. Veo 3.1 GA survives on Google's enterprise platform, the renamed Vertex AI ([Google AI Studio deprecations](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/deprecations)).
- **Kling 4.0 arrived on 16 October.** It offers native 30-second clips, extension to two minutes, 10 keyframes, up to 15 references, and depth-map reference videos that drive an exact camera move ([Kling 4.0 press release](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/kling-ai-launches-kling-4-105300062.html)).
- **Older GPT Image models are being retired.** OpenAI retires gpt-image-1.5, gpt-image-1-mini and chatgpt-image-latest on 1 December ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)).

Any recipe that names a model therefore needs a pinned version.

On Artificial Analysis's blind-vote text-to-video leaderboard (read 8 Nov), the top tier is **Wan 3.0 (Elo 1172), Utopai X (1167), MiniMax H3 (1160), FLUX 3 Max (1156), Seedance 2.5 (1155) and HeyGen Video 1 (1152)**. For comparison, Kling 3.0 1080p Pro is the fixed anchor at 1000, Veo 3.1 scores 963 and LTX-2.5 scores 942 ([AA text-to-video](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/text-to-video)). The image-to-video board matters more for work anchored to real stills. There, **Seedance 2.5 leads at 1158**, ahead of Utopai X, Seedance 2.0 and H3 ([AA image-to-video](https://artificialanalysis.ai/video/leaderboard/image-to-video)). Arena's 6 November image-to-video snapshot, seen only through search snippets, puts the brand-new Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash first ([Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/video/image-to-video)). The boards disagree, leads under about 20 Elo are noise, and none of them tests trades content, so they give a shortlist rather than a winner. H3's architecture has spread: Utopai X, HeyGen Video 1 and Pika H3 Flex are all post-trained on it. That puts near-top realism at several price points, down to HeyGen Video 1's list price of US$0.03 a second at 768p ([HeyGen October 2026](https://www.heygen.com/blog/heygen-october-2026-release)).

Practitioner tests give each model a recognisable character:
- **Veo 3.1:** best lip sync in Kapwing's June test, but a "too polished/staged" look ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/best-ai-video-models-for-realistic-humans/)).
- **Kling:** smoothest motion in the same test, but subject and background details drifted over the clip.
- **Seedance:** reported strongest for product-style shots. ByteDance blocks uploads of photoreal human faces ([Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/bytedance-relaunches-seedance-20-globally-with-restrictions-on-realface-uploads-68605)), and studios sent cease-and-desist letters after the February launch ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/16/bytedance-pledges-fixes-to-seedance-2-0-after-hollywood-copyright-claims)).
- **Gemini Omni 1.2:** the cheap workhorse that can edit. It makes 3–15 s clips, its native output is 720p (1080p and 4K are upsampled), and it supports multi-turn edits that swap background, lighting or aspect ratio while keeping the rest of the scene. Testers rate it weaker at cinematic advertising and photoreal people ([MLQ.ai](https://mlq.ai/news/google-releases-gemini-omni-12-flash-with-15-second-video-and-lower-api-pricing/); [Google AI Studio pricing](https://aistudio.google.com/docs/pricing)).
- **Runway:** in 2026 its value lies in finishing more than generation. Aleph 2 propagates a one-frame edit through a whole shot, SAM3 segments subjects to transparent WebM, it exports ProRes 4444, and it hosts many other vendors' models ([Runway changelog](https://runway.com/changelog)).

For stills, OpenAI holds the top of both blind-vote boards. GPT Image 2.6 Sunburst leads Artificial Analysis at 1259 (about US$0.21 an image at max quality), and GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst led Arena's image-edit board on 6 November ([AA text-to-image](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-image/arena/leaderboard-text); [Arena image edit](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/image/image-edit/single-image)). **Nano Banana 2.1 is the value pick**, at 1176 Elo for US$33.60 per thousand images ([AA text-to-image](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-image/arena/leaderboard-text)). getimg.ai's 25-model realism test is the only systematic one found. In it, GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Nano Banana 2 were the only models rated top on every prompt, Nano Banana 2 "led outright" on homes and architecture, and hands still failed "even on the leaders" ([getimg.ai](https://getimg.ai/blog/ai-realism-test-comparing-realistic-image-generation-models)). GPT Image 2.6 is four days old, and one early tester rolled 2.6 Flare back to 2.5 ([eesel AI](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/chatgpt-images-2-6)), so 2.5 Sunburst remains the safer default for editing real photos. Specialists still earn a place:
- **Midjourney V8.2** for art direction, though it has no public API ([TeamDay](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-image-models-2026)).
- **Recraft V4.1** for native editable SVG at US$0.08 a vector ([Recraft API pricing](https://www.recraft.ai/docs/api-reference/pricing)).
- **FLUX 3** for multi-turn edits that leave every other pixel untouched ([Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328502/20261002/black-forest-labs-launches-flux-3-image-json-bounding-boxes-lock-unchanged-pixels-numerically.htm)).

Adobe MAX runs 10–12 November, so a new Firefly model may land days after this report ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/design-software/adobe-max-2026s-lineup-tells-us-a-lot-about-the-companys-ai-priorities-right-now)).

Upscaling is where much of the AI look gets added after the fact. Faithful modes such as Magnific Precision keep faces, small text and packaging intact. Creative modes "re-imagine skin, texture, and material", which drifts faces and redraws small text ([invideo upscaler comparison](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-image-upscalers/)). SeedVR2 on fal does faithful upscaling for US$0.001 a megapixel ([fal pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing)). Adobe bought Topaz Labs on 25 June 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/adobe-acquires-image-and-video-enhancement-tool-maker-topaz-labs/)), and Topaz's standard plans allow commercial use only for organisations under US$1M revenue ([Topaz pricing](https://www.topazlabs.com/pricing)).

## Credit expiry, not sticker price, decides the monthly bill

### Five ways vendors charge for usage

Platforms differ less in which models they offer than in how they charge for them. For a small agency with uneven weeks, the expiry rule matters more than the headline price. Vendors bill in five ways:
- **Monthly credits that vanish at renewal:** Higgsfield and Adobe.
- **Annual pools:** Magnific.
- **Rollover allowances:** Runway Max for one month; Figma Weave up to 3–4 times the monthly allowance.
- **Pooled dollar budgets:** FLORA.
- **Pay-per-use APIs where nothing expires:** Gemini, fal, BytePlus and Runway's developer API.

The pattern is consistent:
- **Images are cheap everywhere**, at US$3–16 per hundred.
- **Kling 3.0 and Veo with audio** are cheapest on Magnific.

## Anything that says "this is us" must be real

Audiences now punish AI that looks effortless rather than AI as such, and trades are where that penalty is steepest. The survey evidence is stark. In the Harris/4As study released at Cannes in June 2026, **73% said they are less likely to trust an ad they suspect was made with AI, and 63% are less likely to buy** ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/harris-poll-ai-fatigue-less-trust-ai-generated-ads-cannes-lions)). The controlled evidence is more nuanced:
- **Kantar:** 356 AI-made ads performed across the same range as conventional ones, but "when generative AI is obvious or poorly integrated, it can hurt performance" ([Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/effective-creative-principles-remain-the-same-whether-or-not-ads-use-ai-finds-kantar/id/5145503)).
- **Taboola academic study:** AI ads drew a 0.76% click-through rate against 0.65% for human-made ads ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/with-ai-backlash-building-marketers-reconsider-their-approach/)).
- **Patil and Rice (Journal of Business Research, 2026):** across eight studies, disclosing AI use cut click-through by 2.23 percentage points and lowered perceived product quality. **The penalty was stronger for handmade products** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0148296326003899)). A tradesperson's skilled work sits squarely in that category.
- **Real founder photos:** a founder's real photo beat a stock image and lifted consultation sign-ups by about 35% in a single A/B test ([MarketingExperiments](https://marketingexperiments.com/digital-advertising/stock-images-tested)).

The backlash cases follow the same pattern. McDonald's Netherlands pulled its AI Christmas spot after about three days of "AI slop" criticism ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/world/europe/mcdonalds-ai-generated-christmas-advert-social-media-backlash-rcna248590)). Kalshi's chaotic NBA Finals ad worked because AI weirdness was the joke ([Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/the-chaotic-kalshi-ad-during-the-nba-finals-was-made-with-ai-for-2000-the-guy-behind-the-clip-shared-how-he-made-it/)). The mechanism running through all of this is perceived effort and human presence. For a Melbourne electrician whose customers will meet Jimmy on site, a fake face or a fake job photo is the most expensive mistake available.

### Generate-versus-real guide for local service businesses

| Asset | Verdict | How to handle it |
|---|---|---|
| Owner and team (Jimmy, Tony): faces, portraits, talking to camera | **Must be real** | Phone-shot in natural light. Get signed consent covering paid ads, platforms, duration and any AI processing. |
| Completed jobs, switchboards, before/after | **Must be real** | AI is limited to faithful upscaling, exposure and colour, and removing clutter that is not part of the work. A licensed electrician signs off. |
| Van, uniform, livery, premises | **Real** | AI may extend sky or background, or reframe. Never regenerate livery or logos. |
| Reviews, star ratings, testimonials | **Real and verbatim** | Take them from the 85 Google reviews through the claims ledger and set them as live type. Never use AI customers. |
| Licence numbers, badges (e.g. Master Electricians), prices, phone number | **Real data, set as live type** | Never inside generated pixels |
| Service-area streets and landmarks | Real preferred | Generated suburbs only as generic backdrops built from local reference photos, and never captioned as a specific address |
| Explainers (old fuse board vs RCD board, how a safety switch trips) | **Generate or illustrate** | Make them clearly illustrative (vector or hand-drawn), and have an electrician check accuracy |
| Layout plates, paper and grain textures, dusk exteriors | Generating is fine | Nano Banana 2.1 or stock, with no text in the image |
| B-roll inserts (macro wiring, a light switching on, house lights at dusk) | AI-assisted is fine | Image-to-video from real stills where possible, silent, 3–6 s. Skip sparks and fire imagery: it contradicts the landing page's calm-expert tone. |
| Seasonal variants of a real hero shot | AI-assisted | Composite the real van or person over a new sky or background |
| Reframes and extensions of real footage | AI-assisted, with limits | Add only neutral content (wall, sky, room), never extra work |
| Voice-over | Real voice preferred | Jimmy's own voice. A synthetic library voice is acceptable. Clone a voice only with written consent. |
| Music and SFX | Licensed | Use a paid tier that covers digital ads, and record real on-site ambience |

### Craft checklist for a non-AI finish

In 2026 the tells are less about extra fingers and more about an overall impression: poreless skin, hazy over-even light, saturated grades, uniform micro-sharpness, dead eyes, and above all a sameness that could belong to any brand ([Medium/Design Bootcamp](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/aesthetics-in-the-ai-era-visual-web-design-trends-for-2026-5a0f75a10e98)). People are also poor at spotting photoreal AI faces, so a viewer's sense that something is AI comes from the work as a whole, not one defect ([MediaCat UK](https://mediacat.uk/ai-disclosure-when-it-does-and-doesnt-hurt-brands/)). The fixes are craft habits. Google's own Veo guide tells users to define camera, lens, lighting and sound explicitly ([Google DeepMind Veo guide](https://deepmind.google/models/veo/prompt-guide/)). Kalshi's creator generated 300–400 clips to use about 15 ([Business Insider NL](https://www.businessinsider.nl/the-chaotic-kalshi-ad-during-the-nba-finals-was-made-with-ai-for-2000-the-guy-behind-the-clip-shared-how-he-made-it/)). Short shot lengths in that ad were a workaround for continuity failures ([Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/googles-veo-3-ai-slopfest-just-reached-new-heights-2000615508)). The post-production numbers below come from vendor blogs, so treat them as starting points ([invideo post-production](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-video-post-production/); [invideo on plasticky video](https://invideo.io/faq/why-does-ai-generated-video-look-plasticky-or-fake-and/); [invideo on grain](https://invideo.io/faq/does-adding-film-grain-and-blur-to-ai-generated-video/); [invideo on grading](https://invideo.io/faq/how-do-you-color-grade-ai-generated-video-clips-to-look/); [OpenArt on skin](https://openart.ai/blog/midjourney-prompts-for-skin/); [Envato motion trends](https://elements.envato.com/learn/motion-design-trends)).

| Stage | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| References | Keep a pack of 20–40 real photos per client (van, uniforms, sites, owner) and a colour script with exact hex values | Text-only prompts for anything real-world. They default to US panels and left-hand-drive vans. |
| Prompt | Put camera, lens, framing and light first. Name the imperfection ("overcast window light", "handheld phone, slight motion blur"). Ask for visible pores and fine hairs. | Boilerplate like "perfect", "airbrushed", "ultra-detailed", "8K" or "cinematic lighting" |
| Anchor | Use image-to-video from real stills, or hand off from the last frame of real footage. Pass the same reference IDs on every call. | Fresh text-to-video for each shot of the same scene |
| Generate | Work in small batches (about five prompts at a time), overgenerate, and expect to keep about 1 in 20. Log seed and model version. | Accepting first takes |
| Shot design | Hold shots longer only where continuity holds. Cut on story or beat. Use first/last-frame or keyframes to connect shots. | Montages of 1–3 s clips; AI morph or dissolve transitions |
| Upscale | Faithful upscalers for real photos, faces and labels. A light skin-enhancer pass with grain up and sharpness low. | Creative upscalers on faces or text; high sharpness that adds pores or fractal skin |
| Finishing order | Upscale, then soften (0.3–0.6 px), then grade every clip to one reference still, then add moving grain (start around 2–3% and judge in motion), then add motion blur or shake where motion is too clean | Grading before upscaling; halation on phone-style footage; teal-orange grades; untouched saturation (pull it down 10–15%) |
| Sound | Real ambience, natural-paced VO, licensed music | Robotic or rushed synthetic VO; lip-sync drift |
| Type and brand | All text, logos, prices and captions live in Remotion or Figma | Any generated text, signage or logo |
| QA scan | Check frame by frame for waxy skin, hazy bloom, uniform sharpness, centred symmetry, dead eyes, morphing hands or objects, floaty motion, gibberish text, near-miss logos, and a generic look any brand could own | Approving from thumbnails |

## Australian law punishes false impressions, not AI itself

Australia has **no AI-specific advertising or labelling law** as of November 2026. Lawyer Avinash Singh of Astor Legal put it bluntly in July: "There is currently no legal obligation to tell consumers when AI has been used to create or enhance marketing images." What does apply is the Australian Consumer Law's s18 (misleading or deceptive conduct) and s29 (false representations), and an ad is judged on its overall impression ([Cyber Daily](https://www.cyberdaily.au/digital-transformation/13855-legal-trouble-misleading-ai-images-could-lead-to-millions-in-fines)). Disclaimers rarely cure a misleading overall impression ([Lawyers Weekly](https://www.lawyersweekly.com.au/sme-law/44359-moderation-cannot-be-an-afterthought-what-to-know-before-you-post-an-albo-meme)). For conduct from **28 March 2026**, maximum corporate penalties are the greatest of **A$100M, three times the benefit, or 30% of adjusted turnover** ([JWS](https://jws.com.au/what-we-think/double-trouble-penalties-for-breaches-of-competition-and-consumer-laws-have-increased-again/)).

The policy direction is to rely on existing law:
- **Treasury** concluded in October 2025 that the ACL can generally handle AI ([Treasury](https://treasury.gov.au/review/review-ai-and-australian-consumer-law)).
- **The National AI Plan** of 2 December 2025 dropped mandatory guardrails in favour of existing laws ([MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/australia-introduces-a-national-ai-plan-four-things-leaders-need-to-know)).
- **The AANA Code of Ethics review**, which is considering an AI-specific rule, closed submissions in January 2026, and no outcome was found ([Ad Standards](https://adstandards.com.au/article/have-your-say-on-the-aana-code-of-ethics/)).

Enforcement is already landing on fake endorsements. PhotobookShop paid A$39,600 in infringement notices over undisclosed influencer reviews ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/photobookshop-pays-penalties-for-influencer-reviews)). HiSmile was penalised for fake testimonials and then ran glitchy AI ads weeks later ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/decayed-teeth-glitching-mouths-hismile-using-ai-ads-weeks-after-being-fined-for-misleading-content/)).

Ownership and likeness rules raise the stakes on what goes into a model. Australian copyright requires human authorship (*Telstra v Phone Directories*), which "points strongly" to purely AI-generated output attracting **no copyright**. A competitor could copy a raw AI asset, and recording the human creative contribution strengthens the agency's claim ([Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/copyright-ai-and-the-shifting-onus-on-australian-creators)). A statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy has applied since **10 June 2025**, with no proof of damage needed ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/more-privacy-rights/statutory-tort-for-serious-invasions-of-privacy)). Senator Pocock's "My Face, My Rights" deepfake bill would require "express, voluntary and informed" consent, but there is no evidence it has passed ([Pocock](https://www.davidpocock.com.au/new_bill_to_protect_identity_in_deepfake_future)). Drafting consent to that standard now is cheap insurance.

Vendor terms decide what the agency can actually promise a client:
- **Commercial use:** almost every paid plan allows it.
- **IP indemnity:** rare and mostly enterprise-only. It is offered by Adobe Firefly (enterprise contracts), Google Cloud (paid, generally available services, unmodified output), Higgsfield (Enterprise), and Magnific's top or enterprise tier.
- **What indemnity never covers:** no vendor indemnity found covers trademark claims from campaign use. Cover is void when the customer "knew or should have known" the output infringed, and it often lapses once output is modified, including by Photoshop retouching ([Beri](https://www.beri.net/article/best-ai-image-generator-indemnity-firefly-vertex-imagen-openai-getty-shutterstock); [Google Cloud Service Terms](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms)).
- **Licence traps:** FLUX [dev] weights are non-commercial, so using them for client work is likely a breach ([BFL](https://bfl.ai/legal/non-commercial-license-terms)). Companies above US$1M revenue reportedly need Midjourney Pro or Mega to own outputs ([terms.law](https://terms.law/2026/01/15/midjourney-commercial-use-rights-complete-2026-guide/)). Kling keeps a broad reuse licence even on paid plans ([WaveSpeed](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/commercial-compliance/is-kling-seedance-output-safe-for-commercial-use/)). Runway trains on inputs and outputs except on Enterprise ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/runway/runway-terms-of-service/ai-training-license-over-inputs-and-outputs/)).
- **Music:** ElevenLabs' self-serve music rights exclude film, TV and radio ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/eleven-music-model-specific-terms)). Udio is moving to a walled garden whose creations cannot leave the platform ([Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/warner-music-settles-udio-lawsuit-strikes-licensing-deal-with-ai-platform/)).

Platform labels will often appear whether or not the agency discloses:
- **Meta:** from a 1 June 2026 update, it auto-detects third-party AI using industry signals and adds "AI info" inside "About this ad". Ads made with Meta's own tools that include a photorealistic AI person carry the label next to "Sponsored" ([Meta Newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/02/gen-ai-transparency-metas-ads-products/)).
- **Google Ads:** since July it offers an optional AI-label setting, citing EU, India and New York rules rather than Australian ones ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/17257106?hl=en)).
- **TikTok:** it has a self-disclosure toggle for non-Spark ads. **The toggle resets when a campaign is duplicated** ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/about-ad-disclaimers-in-tiktok-ads-manager)).
- **Provenance marks:** C2PA metadata is usually stripped on upload, while SynthID watermarks survive ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-creative-licensing-landmines-agency-client-work)).

The evidence on whether labels hurt performance conflicts. A display field study found labels cut clicks on AI ads, by about 31.5% according to a secondary write-up whose figure is unverified ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/telling-consumers-an-ad-is-ai-generated-cuts-clicks-by-31-percent-study-finds/)). A video study found no decline ([Marketing Dive](https://marketingdive.com/news/ai-disclosure-labels-dont-hurt-ad-performance-heres-what-the-numbers-say/822711/)). Either way, design creative that survives being labelled.

## Two clients, two pipelines, one code-rendered studio

### Canvases design the chain, Claude Code runs it

Node canvases are now standard: Figma Weave, Magnific (formerly Freepik) Spaces, FLORA, Krea Nodes, Runway Workflows and ComfyUI. They are converging on publishing a graph as a simple tool that teammates can fill in, and on exposing it to coding agents. Two of them are already reachable from this studio:
- **Figma Weave:** external clients have been able to run Weave tools since 11 August 2026, and every Weave image and video model has been open through the Figma MCP since 26 October. This needs a paid Weave account, and agents see only published or shared tools ([CreatorsToolbox](https://creatorstoolbox.com/blog/figma-access-video-and-image-models-from-figma-weave-via-the-figma-mcp)).
- **Magnific Spaces:** its List node fans one graph out over many inputs, which gives native variant batches ([Freepik docs](https://www.freepik.es/ai/docs/nodes-and-connections)). Its connector exposes `spaces_run`, `flows_run`, `simulate_cost`, brand kits, `creations_lineage` and `naming_conventions` (observed in this session's connector tool list, 8 Nov 2026).

The others are weaker fits. Comfy Cloud's Team plan costs US$700 a month, and shared workflows are still "coming soon" ([Comfy Cloud pricing](https://comfy.org/cloud/pricing)), which is too heavy for a small team. FLORA lists API and MCP access from its Starter plan ([FLORA pricing](https://www.flora.ai/pricing)), but it would be a new island.

The practical pattern is to design a chain with the art director in Weave or Spaces, freeze it as a published tool or flow, and call it from Claude Code per input row. Run `simulate_cost` or `ads_studio_quote` first, so the studio's approval rule for paid generation always has a number attached. Agency efforts at visual consistency most often break across a roughly 50-asset campaign ([Laffaz](https://laffaz.com/operationalizing-visual-consistency-multi-model-agency-workflows/)). The fix is a constraint system rather than a better generator.

Give every generated asset a sidecar manifest recording:
- client, campaign and variant ID;
- tool, model and version string, prompt, seed and reference IDs;
- parent asset (lineage);
- cost estimate, approver and date.

Keep recipes, specs and brand tokens in git and binaries in Drive. The naming pattern `{client}_{campaign}_{format}_{hook}_{offer}_{v##}_{yyyymmdd}` maps one-to-one onto a variant matrix that drives render props. That single log does three jobs at once: it makes work reproducible, it is evidence for the ACL truth check and any indemnity claim, and it records the human contribution that supports copyright.

### Remotion stays free only up to three staff

Remotion is free for individuals and for-profit companies with **up to three employees**. Above that, a Company License is required, priced at remotion.pro/license; those prices were not captured ([Remotion LICENSE.md](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). It has had official Agent Skills since January 2026 ([Remotion on X](https://x.com/Remotion/status/2013626968386765291)), and the studio's specs, scene library and QA contact sheets already depend on it, so staying is the default while headcount is three or fewer.

HeyGen's **HyperFrames** is the credible exit. It is an open-source HTML-to-MP4 renderer built for agents, with skills for Claude Code and a non-interactive CLI. Its README says Apache 2.0 with no per-render fees, while the marketing site says MIT and Apache 2.0, so read the LICENSE file before relying on either ([GitHub: HyperFrames](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes)). Its strongest argument for this agency is reuse: Alphalink's landing page is HTML and CSS (paper, brush marks, Figtree, Lato and Gochi Hand), so ads could reuse its components directly. Its maturity was not measured. Pilot one landing-derived ad in HyperFrames before a fourth hire, rather than porting the whole studio.

### Alphalink: real footage carries the claim, code carries the brand

The approved 2026 landing page is documentary photography plus hand-made marks on warm paper, the opposite of glossy AI aesthetics. So the pipeline spends money on getting real material and uses generation only around it. Three inputs come before any generation:
- **The raw 20-second fit-off clip, straight from Jimmy's phone.** Work from the original file rather than the CapCut export. Do not remove the watermark with AI inpainting, and keep CapCut out of the paid-ad pipeline because its commercial ad licensing could not be confirmed ([AdMake](https://admakeai.com/blog/best-free-ai-ad-generators-2026)).
- **Full-resolution originals** of the 600–1,600 px site photos.
- **Signed consent** for Jimmy's and Tony's faces and voices.

### City Rock y Ramen: animate the poster, retire the morphs

The current 4.2-second intro packs three separately generated clips into about 1.4 seconds each and joins them with AI morph and dissolve transitions. That reproduces two of the clearest tells: morphing mid-shot, and very short shots used to hide continuity problems. The repo notes that the supplied poster contains third-party characters. Before any model sees it, get written confirmation from NXT Concept that they hold rights to the artwork, characters and music. Never prompt for named IP: vendor indemnities void on knew-or-should-have-known infringement, and Seedance runs IP filters after studio complaints ([Beri](https://www.beri.net/article/best-ai-image-generator-indemnity-firefly-vertex-imagen-openai-getty-shutterstock); [Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/bytedance-relaunches-seedance-20-globally-with-restrictions-on-realface-uploads-68605); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/16/bytedance-pledges-fixes-to-seedance-2-0-after-hollywood-copyright-claims)).

There are three ways to rebuild the intro, in order of preference:
- **Layered 2.5D parallax** of the poster's own layers. It keeps the illustrator's line work pixel-exact and generates no AI frames at all.
- **One continuous five-second keyframed generation**, trimmed to 4.2 s. Kling 4.0 takes up to 10 keyframes; Seedance 2.5 and Wan 3.0 take first and last frames.
- **Keep three shots but join them with designed cuts** in Remotion: the existing flash, wipe and zoom transitions, timed to the kick drum.

If Ultra credits are already paid for, this is the account where Seedance atmosphere plates on Higgsfield make the most sense.

## Conclusion

The generator is no longer the moat. When the same few models sit behind every aggregator and swap leaderboard places monthly, the durable assets are elsewhere: the trust layer of real people and real jobs, a code-rendered brand system that makes text and layout exact, and a provenance log that serves reproducibility, ACL defence and copyright at the same time. That changes how to buy. The question stops being "which platform has the best model" and becomes "which meter wastes least on our usage pattern". For a real-first agency the answer is long-lived credits plus pay-per-use, with every connector driven from one Claude Code loop. Because that loop is MCP-based, switching vendors later costs a recipe edit, not a retraining, so there is no reason to prepay a year for promotional extras.

The second implication is strategic. As platforms label AI ads by default and audiences discount anything that feels effortless, an agency that can say "the people and jobs are real; AI only builds the set" holds a positioning that pure-AI shops cannot copy. That is worth more to a Melbourne electrician than any per-clip saving. Re-run the cost comparison quarterly, pin model versions per campaign, and treat each vendor's renewal date, not its promotion deadline, as the real decision point.
